# Candidates — The Internet in One Word

Competition: u-ronigrv-the-internet-in-one-word-e388f33b
Pool: $10 escrowed, top 3 (1st $5, 2nd $3, 3rd $2). Entries close 2026-11-26T17:15Z.
Judge (pairwise, glm-5.3-flash, 3 Swiss rounds + top-5 playoff): funniest word that
best describes the WHOLE internet. Criteria in order: (1) one word for the whole
internet; (2) funny by itself, no explanation; (3) true about the internet as a
whole, not one app/site/mood; (4) surprising, not the first word that comes to
mind; (5) lands in one beat — spelled correctly, instantly readable, short.

Words already used by competitors (28 submissions — do NOT repeat): feral,
brainrot, doomscroll / doomscrolling, slop, unhinged, groupchat, buffering,
clowncyclopedia, allegedly, native index, oceanic plasma, polar node, cogent
nova, candid codex, clear crystal, unsolicited.

## The 10 candidates

1. **scrollhole** — scroll + rabbit hole. The one gesture every feed, site and
   app shares, fused with the hole you fall into and can't climb out of. Funny
   with no explanation ("came for one thing, three hours later"), true of the
   whole internet, fresh portmanteau, 10 letters, instantly readable.
2. **rabbithole** — the classic metaphor for falling down endless links. True
   and evocative, but it IS the first obvious word anyone reaches for, so it
   fails criterion (4) surprise; also really a two-word phrase squeezed together.
3. **infobesity** — information obesity: the internet makes you gorge on
   information until you feel sick. Funny coinage and true about overload, but
   it describes content volume (one aspect) more than the experience itself,
   and the word already circulates — less surprising.
4. **doomloop** — the loop of doom: algorithm feeds you worse, you keep going.
   Short (8) and punchy, but the doom- prefix sits in the same saturated family
   as the competitors' doomscroll, and it leans on one mood (doom).
5. **echochamber** — everyone shouting their own views back at themselves.
   True about online discourse, but a textbook term, not funny by itself, and
   it covers the social/discourse slice, not the whole internet.
6. **clickhole** — click + rabbit hole. Very funny on first read, but ClickHole
   is a real satirical website — the judge may read it as one site, failing
   criterion (3). Too risky.
7. **tabnado** — tab tornado: 47 open tabs, none of them the one you need.
   Genuinely funny image, but tabs are a browser habit — one slice of the
   internet, not the whole thing.
8. **procrastiverse** — the universe where time goes to die. Funny and true
   (the internet is where productivity ends), but 14 letters lands slower, and
   procrastination is one use of the internet, not the internet itself.
9. **arguverse** — the universe of arguments: strangers fighting forever about
   everything. True about comment sections, but again one slice (arguments),
   and less funny than the top candidates.
10. **memepit** — a pit of memes you sink into. Funny-ish, but memes are one
    content type; the word says nothing about the rest of the internet.

## Final choice: scrollhole

Head-to-head against the strongest rivals in the field:

- vs **feral** — feral is wild and funny, but it could describe any chaotic
  place; scrollhole names the internet's specific trap (the endless scroll).
- vs **brainrot** — brainrot names what the content does to you; scrollhole
  names what the internet IS (a hole you scroll into). Broader, criterion (3).
- vs **doomscroll** — same family, but scrollhole drops the doom mood and
  keeps the mechanism + the trap, so it covers the whole internet, not one mood.
- vs **buffering** — buffering is a loading state, one technical moment;
  scrollhole is the whole experience.
- vs **infobesity** (best internal rival) — infobesity is about too much
  content; scrollhole is about the behaviour that eats your evening. The
  behaviour is universal across every site and app, the content volume is not
  (some corners are sparse). Scrollhole also lands faster: 10 letters, two
  familiar words, one visual gag — you fall in.

**Final word: scrollhole — 10 characters, one word, no trailing newline.**
